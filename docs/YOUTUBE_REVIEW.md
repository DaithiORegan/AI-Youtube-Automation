# YouTube, rights and editorial review

Official guidance checked **13 November 2026**:

- [Altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [Channel monetisation policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [Made for kids audience decisions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076)

YouTube requires disclosure for certain meaningful synthetic/altered content that appears realistic, including making real people appear to say/do something they did not, altering real events/places, or generating realistic events that did not happen. Clearly stylised fictional animation is treated differently from realistic synthetic footage. Apply the current upload question to the actual video; do not assume that every use of AI requires the same label, or that fictional narration is exempt if the visuals misleadingly appear real.

The description template transparently identifies original fiction and local AI-assisted illustrations/narration. That description is **not a replacement** for YouTube's disclosure setting when the setting is required. Review any synthetic music or other media under the current guidance as well.

AI use alone does not decide monetisation. YouTube's inauthentic-content policy includes repetitive or mass-produced content. Changing a title, colour, voice or a few prompts does not make formulaic episodes meaningfully original. Reused-content, copyright and other rules also apply. This tool cannot certify eligibility or guarantee monetisation, recommendations, views or approval.

## Before a public upload

1. **Story:** read it, watch it and remove filler, contradictions, copied plot details and misleading real-world claims. Label it as fiction. Vary the substance of episodes, not just their packaging.
2. **Visuals:** check every shot for consistent faces/costume, malformed hands, accidental logos/text, distracting crops, and unwanted real-person resemblance. Prompt bibles are not a guarantee of identity consistency.
3. **Audio:** use a voice/model with suitable terms. Do not imitate a recognisable person without the necessary permissions. Check pronunciation, rhythm, captions and music balance.
4. **Rights:** review the checkpoint's model card/licence, Kokoro/voice terms, imported illustrations and any music. “Free download” is not a universal commercial-use licence. Keep provenance and receipts/permission records where relevant.
5. **Audience:** animation is not automatically “made for kids” or automatically general/adult content. Choose based on the actual subject, presentation and intended audience.
6. **Upload:** start private; confirm synthetic-content disclosures as applicable, title accuracy, copyright/restriction notices, processing quality, thumbnail and subtitles.

The studio records review confirmations against the current project, assets and final video hash. It never presses Publish. If the project or scene assets change, rerender and review again. The approval file is your assertion, not a platform or legal certificate.
